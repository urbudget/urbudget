Getting started with envelope budgeting

Envelope budgeting means giving the money you have right now a job, then spending from those jobs. Five steps get you there, and UrBudget's getting-started checklist walks you through the same ones.

How does envelope budgeting work?

Budget the money you have, not the money you expect. Everything in your accounts goes into a category, like rent, groceries or the car insurance that's due in March, until nothing is left unassigned. When you spend, it comes out of its category. When a category runs short, you move money over from another one. Whatever is left carries into next month.

That's the whole method. It's often called the envelope system, after the days when people kept cash for each kind of spending in labeled envelopes, and also zero-based budgeting, because you keep assigning until zero dollars are left without a job. The zero-based budgeting guide goes deeper into the method and how it compares with 50/30/20.

Your first budget, worked through

Say you start on October 20 with $2,900 in checking and $1,000 in savings, and your Visa card still owes $300 from last month. Your paycheck comes on the 1st. The money you have to assign is the $3,900 in checking and savings; the $300 card balance is a debt, not money.

CategoryAssignedWhy this amount
Rent$1,450Due November 1, before the next paycheck clears
Visa payment$300Covers the balance from before you started
Groceries$520About $130 a week
Utilities$230Power, water and internet
Car insurance$95$570 is due in March: six months, $95 a month
Dining out$180Fun money you've decided you can afford
Emergency fund$1,125Everything left over
Total$3,900Unassigned: $0

Rent comes first because it's due first. The card's old $300 gets money now, so the next statement is covered. The irregular bill gets a monthly slice instead of a surprise in March. What's left goes to savings, because unassigned money tends to get spent.

Two weeks in, groceries have cost $560, so that category shows $40 overspent. You move $40 from Dining out, which drops to $140. Nothing else changes, and the budget still adds up to $3,900. On November 1 the paycheck arrives and you assign it the same way, starting with what's due before the paycheck after it.

Prefer to try this in a spreadsheet first? The free envelope budget template works the same way, with Assigned, Spent and Available for each category.

Five steps to set it up

These are the same steps whatever envelope app you use. The notes in each one say where it lives in UrBudget, whose getting-started checklist walks you through them.

  1. Add your accounts. Add each checking and savings account and each credit card, with today's balance. Investments, a mortgage or a car can be tracking accounts: they count toward your net worth without being budgeted. The total in your budget accounts is the money you have to assign.
  2. Give your money a job. In the budget, type amounts in the Assigned column until Unassigned reaches zero. Start with what's due before your next paycheck. Quick assign can fill categories from their targets or repeat last month. If there isn't enough to go around, it's better to find out now than on the due date.
  3. Record your spending. Add transactions as you spend (amounts can be sums, like 42.10+8), connect a supported US or Canadian bank through SimpleFIN, or download a statement file from your bank, as CSV, OFX, QFX or QIF, and import it. You see each row before it's added, duplicates are skipped, and rows that match ones you entered are paired. Scheduled transactions add your paycheck, rent and subscriptions when they're due.
  4. Plan for bigger bills. Bills that don't come every month, like car insurance, gifts or a yearly subscription, are what break most budgets. Give each one a target, such as an amount by a date, and UrBudget works out what to set aside each month so the money is there when the bill is.
  5. Cover overspending. You'll overspend somewhere; everyone does. When a category's Available turns red, click it and move money over from a category that has some left. The budget isn't broken. It's showing you what the overspending costs, and where it comes from.

Choose how transactions come in

Typing transactions and importing bank files work without a bank connection. Optional SimpleFIN bank sync needs its own subscription, separate from UrBudget's device sync plan. Once connected, press Sync and review what arrived. If you start with today's balance, skip older transactions already included in it; to bring in history, use the balance immediately before your first imported transaction. Check bank availability and set up SimpleFIN.

Credit cards

When you buy something on a card, the money moves from the spending category into the card's payment category, so the payment is covered when the bill comes. When you pay the card from checking, that category goes down with it.

A weekly routine

Ten minutes a week is enough. Sync, import or enter what you spent, check that each transaction has the right category, assign any money that came in, and cover anything that went red. At the start of a month, assign the new month's money.
