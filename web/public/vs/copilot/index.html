UrBudget vs Copilot Money

Copilot connects to your US bank accounts and sorts your spending for you, on iPhone, iPad, Mac and the web. UrBudget is an envelope budget for Windows and Mac: you give the money you have a job, in a file on your own computer, with bank connections optional.

Tracking first, or budgeting first

Copilot is built to need as little of your time as possible. Transactions arrive from your banks, its AI categorizes them and learns your habits over time, and the app shows spending, subscriptions, investments and net worth. Its budget is a spending limit per category. Copilot's own help says it isn't built as a zero-based budgeting tool and doesn't automatically tie your budget to your income; you set the total yourself.

UrBudget asks for a few minutes a week instead. You fetch transactions through optional SimpleFIN for supported US and Canadian banks, or import statement files, then assign the money that's in your accounts to categories, and move money between categories when plans change. In exchange, you always know what each dollar is for before you spend it, and your budget never depends on a bank connection.

What it costs over time

Over five years, Copilot comes to $475 on the yearly plan, or $780 paid monthly. UrBudget is free, or $180 if you also keep sync on the whole time.

Which one fits you

Copilot, if you

  • use an iPhone, iPad or Mac and bank in the US
  • want transactions to arrive and sort themselves, with little upkeep
  • want investments and net worth next to your spending
  • want spending limits per category rather than assigning every dollar

UrBudget, if you

  • use Windows, or bank outside the US
  • want envelope budgeting, where every dollar has a job before you spend it
  • would rather not connect your bank accounts to an app
  • want a free app instead of $95 a year

If you want an automatic tracker on your iPhone, Copilot is built for exactly that, and UrBudget is currently a desktop app with bank sync you run and review yourself. If you want to plan your money before you spend it, at your computer, UrBudget is built for that.

Moving from Copilot to UrBudget

Copilot exports your transactions as a CSV file with the date, name, amount, category, account and notes of each one. Budgets don't come with it. UrBudget imports bank statement files into one account at a time.

  1. Export your transactions. On iPhone, go to Settings, then Account, then Export your transactions; that file has every account in it. On the web, Mac or iPad, you can export just the transactions you've filtered in the Transactions tab. Either way you need one file per account for UrBudget, so filter to one account, or split the full file by its account column in a spreadsheet.
  2. Add the account in UrBudget. The opening balance is the starting point before any transactions you add. To start fresh, use today's balance and import only newer transactions. To bring the history, use the balance from just before the file's first transaction.
  3. Import the file and check the signs. In the account's register, choose Import file. UrBudget reads the date, the payee from the Name column, the amount and the notes. It reads a negative amount as money out, so look at a few purchases in the preview before you save. If they show as money in, tick Purchases are positive (UrBudget 1.0.3 and later) and preview again, or use your bank's OFX or QFX file for that account instead.
  4. Give your money a job. Categories don't come over, so create yours and assign what's in your accounts until nothing is left unassigned. From then on, download statement files from your bank and import them the same way.
