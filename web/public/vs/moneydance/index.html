UrBudget vs Moneydance

Moneydance is a full personal finance manager in the Quicken mold: accounts, investments, bill reminders, reports and category budgets. UrBudget does one job, envelope budgeting, for free. Both keep your data in a file on your own computer.

What it costs over time

Moneydance's license is $65 once and covers the next major version too. If you add Moneydance+ for bank downloads, five years comes to $265 on the yearly add-on, or $450 on the yearly subscription that includes the program. Moneydance's sync is part of the app and runs through your own Dropbox or iCloud.

UrBudget is free. With sync on for five years, it comes to $180.

Which one fits you

Moneydance, if you

  • want one app for everything: investments, loans, several currencies and bills
  • want transactions downloaded from your bank, through Direct Connect or Moneydance+ in the US and Canada
  • use Linux, or want to enter transactions on your phone
  • are bringing years of Quicken or Microsoft Money history; Moneydance has import guides for both

UrBudget, if you

  • want envelope budgeting: every dollar gets a job, and you move money when plans change
  • want a budget, not a full finance manager, for free
  • would rather not connect a budgeting app to your bank
  • are leaving YNAB and want your whole budget to come over in one click

These are different kinds of software. If you track investments or more than one currency, Moneydance does far more. If what you want is a budget that tells you what each dollar is for before you spend it, that's what UrBudget is built for.

Moving from Moneydance to UrBudget

UrBudget can't open a Moneydance file, but Moneydance exports QIF files, and UrBudget imports QIF files for bank, credit-card and cash accounts, one account at a time.

  1. Export each account as QIF. In Moneydance, choose File > Export, pick QIF, select a single account rather than All Accounts, choose the date range, and save. Repeat for each bank, credit-card and cash account.
  2. Add the account in UrBudget. Use its balance from before the first transaction in the file. Investments, loans and other accounts you don't budget can be tracking accounts with today's balance; their history stays in Moneydance.
  3. Import the file. Choose Import file in the account's register and pick the QIF file and the date order it uses. Not sure which? Open the file in a text editor: each date is on a line starting with D. You see every row before it's saved.
  4. Set up your categories. UrBudget reads each transaction's date, payee, memo and amount; Moneydance's categories and split details don't come over. Make categories for what you spend on, add targets for bills that don't come every month, and assign the money in your accounts until Unassigned reaches zero.
  5. Import from your bank from now on. Download OFX or QFX files from your bank and import them. Overlapping files are safe: rows you've already imported are marked as duplicates.

Each import file can hold up to 10,000 transactions and 20 MB, so export a long history in a few date ranges. If a QIF file won't import, download OFX or QFX files from your bank for the same period instead.
