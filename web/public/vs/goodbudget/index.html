UrBudget vs Goodbudget

Both use envelopes: you put money into them up front, and spending comes out of them. Goodbudget is an online service for the web and phones, with a free plan and a Premium subscription. UrBudget is a desktop app for Windows and Mac that keeps your budget in a file on your computer.

What it costs over time

Goodbudget's free plan costs nothing for as long as your budget fits its limits. Premium removes the limits and adds bank sync for $80 a year; over five years that's $400, or $600 paying monthly. UrBudget is free, or $180 over five years if you keep sync on the whole time.

Which one fits you

Goodbudget, if you

  • can live within the free plan's 20 envelopes and one account
  • budget on your phone as you spend, or share envelopes with a partner on their phone
  • want transactions from a US bank to arrive on their own (Premium)
  • use a Chromebook or another computer that isn't Windows or a Mac

UrBudget, if you

  • budget at your computer and want a desktop app that works offline
  • want your budget in a file you own rather than in an online account
  • need more envelopes and accounts than the free plan, without $80 a year
  • want targets, credit-card payment categories, a loan planner and twelve reports
  • are leaving YNAB and want your whole budget to come over in one click

If your budget fits the free plan, or you and a partner both budget from your phones, Goodbudget is hard to beat. UrBudget is for people who'd rather budget at a desk and keep the data themselves, with no limits on envelopes or accounts.

Moving from Goodbudget to UrBudget

UrBudget has no Goodbudget importer, and Goodbudget exports transactions but not envelope amounts. Your envelopes take a few minutes to set up by hand; transactions can come over as a CSV file, one account at a time.

  1. Export your transactions. On Goodbudget's website, use the Export CSV link above the Transactions list. The export saves what the list shows, so filter it to one account first and repeat for each account.
  2. Tidy the file. Open it in a spreadsheet and delete rows that only moved money between envelopes, such as fills; they aren't bank transactions. UrBudget reads a Date column, a payee column called Name or Payee, an Amount column and Notes as the memo, so rename any header that differs. Spending should show as negative amounts; if it doesn't, tick Purchases are positive when you import. Save as CSV.
  3. Add the account and import. In UrBudget, add the account with its balance from before the first transaction in the file. Choose Import file in its register, pick the file, and choose the date order your Goodbudget household uses (month/day/year or day/month/year). Check the rows before saving.
  4. Set up your envelopes. Make a category for each envelope and assign it what the envelope holds in Goodbudget today, until Unassigned reaches zero. Annual and goal envelopes become categories with a target, and UrBudget works out what to set aside each month.
  5. Replace bank sync with statement files. Download OFX or QFX files from your bank and import them into each account. Overlapping files are safe: rows you've already imported are marked as duplicates.

If you pay for Premium, cancel it when you're done. Goodbudget keeps your household on Premium until the paid period ends, then turns it into a free household. Export everything you want to keep before then, since the free plan includes only 1 year of transaction history.
