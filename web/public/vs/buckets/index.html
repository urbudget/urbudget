UrBudget vs Buckets

Both are desktop envelope budgets that keep your budget in a file on your own computer. Buckets costs $64 once, runs on Linux too and shares a budget between people. UrBudget is free and brings YNAB-style tools, with optional sync that keeps your computers the same on its own.

What it costs over time

UrBudget is free and open source. Buckets is $64 once. Buckets' license covers its current 0.x versions and version 1; Buckets says a version 2, if one ever comes, would likely have a small upgrade fee.

Sync is where UrBudget can cost more. Kept on for five years, its $3 a month comes to $180. Buckets has no monthly fee, and its sharing tools come with the app, but you merge changes from other devices by hand.

Which one fits you

Buckets, if you

  • use Linux, or want to glance at balances on your phone
  • share one budget with a partner who uses their own computer
  • want to import transactions through a recorded bank macro; both apps also support SimpleFIN
  • still have a YNAB 4 budget to bring over
  • would rather use the app in a language other than English

UrBudget, if you

  • want a free app instead of paying $64
  • use two computers and want sync to keep them the same, with nothing to merge by hand
  • are leaving the current YNAB and want a one-click import, checked month by month, that you can repeat while you decide
  • want targets, credit-card payment categories, Quick assign, a loan planner and twelve reports

If you're on Linux, budget with someone on another computer, or want the app in your own language, Buckets is the better choice. UrBudget is for people on Windows or a Mac who want YNAB-style tools for free.

Moving from Buckets to UrBudget

UrBudget can't open a Buckets file, and Buckets has no export that carries a whole budget. Setting up your accounts and envelopes takes a few minutes by hand. Past transactions can come over as CSV, one account at a time, if you want them.

  1. Keep a copy from Buckets. Nothing you do in UrBudget changes your Buckets file. Export your transactions as CSV from Buckets anyway, so you have them in a form any spreadsheet can open.
  2. Add your accounts. In UrBudget, add each account from Buckets' Accounts tab with today's balance. Accounts you kept off budget in Buckets, like a mortgage, can be tracking accounts: they count toward net worth without being budgeted.
  3. Turn buckets into categories. Make a category for each bucket and assign it what the bucket holds today, until Unassigned reaches zero. A bucket that saves toward an amount by a date becomes a category with a target. For a credit card you're paying down, the card's payment category does the job of Buckets' debt payment bucket.
  4. Import from your bank from now on. Download statement files from your bank, as OFX or QFX if it offers them, and choose Import file in each account's register. You see every row before it's saved, and rows you've already imported are marked as duplicates.

Bringing past transactions

If you want older transactions in UrBudget's reports, import them account by account. UrBudget needs a header row with Date, Payee (or Name, Merchant or Description) and Amount. Money out should be negative; if the file has it the other way round, tick Purchases are positive when you import (UrBudget 1.0.3 and later, which also reads currency symbols). Rename any column Buckets names differently, keep one account per file, and save it as CSV. Start that account at its balance from before the first transaction you import, not today's.
