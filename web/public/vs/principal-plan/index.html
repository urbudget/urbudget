UrBudget vs Principal Plan

Both are envelope budgets for Windows and Mac that keep your budget in a file on your own computer, work offline and never ask for a bank login. Principal Plan has six languages and a phone app that shows your envelope balances. UrBudget is free and can keep two computers in sync.

What it costs over time

Over five years, Principal Plan's yearly license comes to $99.95 and the monthly one to $119.40. Its Forever license is $39.99 once. UrBudget is free.

UrBudget's sync adds $3 a month if you turn it on, which makes $180 over five years. Principal Plan has no sync between computers to set against that, and its phone app is free.

Which one fits you

Principal Plan, if you

  • want the app in German, Spanish, French, Italian or Portuguese
  • want to check envelope balances on your phone while you shop
  • like applying a whole paycheck split with one click, or having the app watch your downloads folder for new bank files

UrBudget, if you

  • want a free app instead of paying $39.99
  • use two computers and want sync to keep them the same
  • are leaving YNAB and want your budget, targets and history to come over in one click
  • want targets, credit-card payment categories, Quick assign, a loan planner and twelve reports

These two are close: both are desktop envelope budgets that work offline. UrBudget also offers optional SimpleFIN bank connections for supported US and Canadian banks. The choice comes down to price, the phone app, the language you want, and whether you budget on more than one computer.

Moving from Principal Plan to UrBudget

UrBudget can't open a Principal Plan file, but Principal Plan exports each account's ledger as CSV, and UrBudget imports CSV one account at a time. Envelopes you set up again by hand.

  1. Export each account's ledger. In Principal Plan, open an Account Ledger, set the filter to show all transactions (the export saves only what the ledger shows), and choose Export Ledger. Repeat for each account.
  2. Rename three columns. Open each file in a spreadsheet. UrBudget looks for Date, Payee and either Amount or Outflow and Inflow. Principal Plan's ledger calls the payee and amount columns Payable to, Payment and Deposit, so rename them Payee, Outflow and Inflow. Where there's no amount, leave the cell empty rather than writing a dash; currency symbols are fine in UrBudget 1.0.3 and later. Save as CSV.
  3. Add the account and import. In UrBudget, add the account with its balance from before the first transaction in the file. Choose Import file in its register, pick the file and the date order it uses, and check the rows before saving.
  4. Set up your envelopes. Make a category for each envelope, grouping nested ones, and assign each what it holds in Principal Plan today until Unassigned reaches zero. Recreate recurring templates, like your paycheck and bills, as scheduled transactions.
  5. Import from your bank from now on. Keep downloading CSV, OFX, QFX or QIF files from your bank, as you did for Principal Plan. Overlapping files are safe: rows you've already imported are marked as duplicates.

Don't need your history? Skip the export: add each account with today's balance, then set up your envelopes.
