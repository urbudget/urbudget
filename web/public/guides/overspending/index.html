What to do when you overspend

Overspending doesn't mean the budget failed. A plan met real life, and the budget is showing you what that cost. The fix takes a minute: move money from somewhere else, then decide whether the plan needs to change.

What overspending is

A category is overspent when its available amount drops below zero: you spent more than you set aside for it. The money still came from somewhere, your account, so the negative number is money that was meant for something else. Covering it means choosing what that something else is.

Cover it from another category

Say Groceries had $500 this month and you spent $560, so it shows −$60. Dining out still has $90. Move $60 from Dining out to Groceries:

CategoryBeforeMoveAfter
Groceries−$60+$60$0
Dining out$90−$60$30
Both together$30$30

Nothing was created or lost. The two categories held $30 between them before and after. The move puts the shortfall where you choose, instead of leaving it to land somewhere by accident.

Where to take it from

  • Money you haven't assigned yet, if there is any.
  • A category with money left that matters less this month, such as dining out or entertainment.
  • A savings goal, if the overspending was worth it. Slowing a goal on purpose is better than pretending the money is still there.
  • Not a bill that's due soon, and not a sinking fund you'll need before you can refill it.

Cash and credit card overspending

How you paid changes what overspending means.

QuestionCash overspendingCredit card overspending
How you paidDebit card, cash, or anything that takes money from your account nowA credit card
ExampleGroceries had $500; you spent $560 with a debit cardDining out had $50; you put an $80 dinner on the card
What it means$60 left your account that the budget had meant for something elseOnly $50 could be set aside for the card's payment; $30 of the charge has nothing set aside
If you cover itGroceries is back to zeroAll $80 is set aside for the card's payment, so the bill is covered
If you don'tNext month starts $60 short: there's $60 less to assignThe $30 stays on the card as debt with nothing set aside to pay it

For how card spending moves money toward the card's payment, see credit cards in an envelope budget.

What happens if you don't cover it

An overspent category starts the next month at zero, but the shortfall doesn't vanish:

  • Cash overspending comes out of next month. The money is already gone from your account, so next month you have that much less to assign. In the example, next month starts $60 short.
  • Credit card overspending becomes debt. The card's payment category holds $30 less than the card's new charges. That $30 has to come from somewhere later, or it stays on the card and may cost interest.

Covering it now uses the same money as covering it later. Doing it now keeps the budget honest about what you have left this month.

Why it keeps happening, and how to stop it

Overspending once is normal. Overspending in the same category every month means the plan is wrong, not you.

  1. The amount is too low. Compare it with what you really spend. If Groceries was $560, $545 and $590 in the last three months, that's $1,695, or $565 a month. Budget $565, or decide where you'll cut and budget $500 on purpose.
  2. It isn't a monthly cost. A yearly bill paid from a monthly category overspends it in the month the bill arrives. Give the bill its own sinking fund.
  3. You find out too late. If you record spending once a month, you've overspent before you know it. Record or import transactions once a week, and look at a category before a big purchase.
  4. The card makes it easy. Spending on a credit card doesn't feel like money leaving. Check the category's available amount before you pay, not after.
  5. There's no room for surprises. Some costs fit no category. A small category for them, say $50 a month, takes the odd surprise without breaking another plan.

In UrBudget

  • Overspending is marked. An overspent category's Available amount turns red, and overspending on a credit card adds a card icon. The Overspent tab above the categories lists every overspent category.
  • Cover it in two clicks. Select the red amount. The amount to cover is filled in, with Unassigned as the source; pick a category instead if you'd rather take it from one, and choose Move. The message that follows has Undo.
  • Quick assign. Underfunded also covers overspending. Applied to all your categories, it covers overspent ones first and never assigns more than Unassigned holds.
  • Rollover. What's left in a category carries into next month. Uncovered cash overspending lowers next month's Unassigned; uncovered card overspending stays on the card, and the category starts the next month at 0.00.
  • Cash first. When a category has both cash and card spending and runs short, its money pays for the cash spending first, and the shortfall counts as card overspending.
  • Find the cause. Select a category's Activity amount to see the transactions behind it. Quick assign's Average spent and Spent last month set assignments from real spending, and the Spending by category report compares the latest month with the average of the months before it.
